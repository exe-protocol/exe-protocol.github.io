Exe Protocol specs

Exe Protocol is a policy-governed execution layer for apps, wallets and agents. Rails like x402, cards or Stripe move the money; Exe Protocol makes each payment smarter: fees priced by verified signal (XPR), within budgets set in advance, settled net. Exeswap is the first app built on Exe Protocol. Exeswap is a working prototype: every figure on this page is simulated unless marked measured or sourced.

Prototype · every figure simulated unless marked

Positioning

Rails move the money. Today's rails are blind to the value a payer's activity creates: fees follow the transaction, or at best its volume. Exe Protocol decides what each payment costs by signal, giving the most fee redemption to the strongest verified signal.

As AI agents grow more capable, the payments they make need to become smarter too, so apps, agents and wallets get the most value: Exe Protocol values the signal agents generate and applies that value at the moment of payment.

Exe Protocol is a horizontal layer that makes spending signal-aware: apps and their agents sit above it, payment rails and chains below.

Layer What it does Examples
Apps and their agents Use Exe Protocol through an SDK (intent) Trading apps, Telegram apps, AI agents and services

x402 is the pipe, not the rival: it moves money between agents and does nothing else. Exe Protocol adds what x402 does not have: fees priced by the payer’s signal, budgets and policy decided before each payment, a shared pool that funds redemption, and settlement netted across all agents. Working with any rail is a statement of intent until built.

Mechanism

Exe Protocol makes every payment smarter. Faster and cheaper follow from that.

  • Priced by signal. A wallet’s XPR (Exe Protocol Rate), earned through verified on-chain activity, decides how much of each fee is redeemed. At Tier 1 with the pool at full strength, XPR 0.60 redeems 60%. Paymasters elsewhere subsidise blindly; Exe pays strong signal most.
  • Funded by a shared pool. Redemption comes first from the payer’s own fees (up to 70% of the protocol fee they paid), then from the Sponsorship Pool. In every stress we tested, 9 of 9 over 24 months each, the pool never ran dry.
  • Decided before it happens. Pre-funded budgets, policy and tiers approve each payment at the quote. A rogue agent cannot spend beyond its budget. Measured in the prototype: 0.054 ms to decide the fee relief.
  • Settled net. Payments are recorded the moment they are approved; at each batch only what each agent owes or is owed overall goes on-chain. One fingerprint per batch lets any agent check its own balance.

The loop, and how far each step is proven:

  1. Strong verified signal gives a higher XPR. Design.
  2. A higher XPR redeems more of each fee, from the payer’s own fees and the shared pool. Tested in simulation.
  3. Rewarding the most valuable activity is designed to attract more of it. Hypothesis, for a pilot.
  4. More activity means more fees funding redemption, and netting saves more as payments per agent rise. Simulated.

Who’s who and who pays

Party What it is What it pays
Wallet (person or agent) Holds an XPR. The XPR belongs to the wallet, is calculated the same way everywhere and is designed to travel across apps The protocol fee (0.10% on low-fee chains, 0.25% on Ethereum) plus gas, less what its XPR redeems
App Integrates Exe Protocol; our client commercially Nothing is required from apps for fee redemption. An app may add a budget so XPR also discounts its own fee
Agent Software with its own wallet and budget, run by an app or by its users The payer pays. The fee falls on whoever funds the agent’s budget
Exe Protocol Runs the policy, the pool and the ledger Puts 70% of its revenue into the pool; 30% goes to operations

Each wallet keeps its own XPR, including each agent’s wallet; apps see the totals. New wallets share only a small, capped part of the pool, so spinning up fresh wallets or agents cannot farm it.

App funding

  • The pool: funded by 70% of Exe Protocol’s revenue, from Exeswap’s fees and the protocol fees that apps’ users pay. Users’ own money never enters it.
  • One account per app: each app’s users draw on the revenue they generate, so no app subsidises another. Exe Protocol starts each new app’s account with about 1.9 months of its expected income; a fifth of that is a buffer that is never spent.
  • What an app pays: nothing is required for fee redemption. On a low-fee chain, 70% of the protocol fee covers what users’ XPR earns with room to spare. On an expensive chain gas dominates, for every app.
  • Optional app budgets: an app can fund a budget so XPR also discounts its own fee. That money goes to its users under the same rules.
  • Surplus: held for $XPT buybacks once legally cleared. Buybacks are off until legal advice.

Agent payments

x402 moves the money; Exe decides what it costs, by signal. Every figure below is simulated for one day: 1,000 agents, 1,000,000 payments a day mostly to popular services, settled hourly.

x402 alone x402 with Exe Protocol
Payment accepted Instantly, by a signed voucher (batch scheme) Instantly, by policy against a pre-funded budget
Fee priced by the payer’s signal No Yes: XPR redeems the fee, to zero at XPR 1.00
Protocol fee None 0.10%, less what XPR redeems
Deposits to pay 200 different agents 200, one channel each 1 budget
On-chain transactions a day 546,742 settling per buyer–seller pair 24,000 netted (22.8× fewer)
Facilitator fees a day, at Coinbase’s $0.001 per on-chain transaction About $1,000 paying one by one; about $547 per pair About $24
  • Speed, plainly: any design that approves payments off-chain decides as fast as the network allows, x402’s batch scheme included. Exe Protocol’s edge is what grows with volume: one budget for every counterparty, settlement netted across all agents, and a fee the payer’s own activity redeems.
  • Checkable settlement: each batch publishes one fingerprint. Any of 1,000 agents checks its own net position with 10 hashes (320 bytes).
  • Custody, planned: each agent’s budget sits in an escrow contract the agent controls. A batch settles only payments the agent signed, and unspent funds can be withdrawn. The prototype still settles through one settlement account, holding funds for the payee for at most one batch interval.
