Exeswap specs

Exeswap is the first app built on Exe Protocol, a policy-governed execution layer for apps, wallets and agents. Exeswap is a DEX aggregator where the user’s verified signal (XPR) decides how much of each trade’s fee is redeemed for that user. Exe Protocol is the horizontal layer; Exeswap is the first vertical app built on it, using the same rules other client apps and agents are designed to use through an SDK. It is a working prototype: every figure here comes from simulation, not live trading.

What it is

Exeswap finds a route for each trade across exchanges, like any aggregator. Our aim is the cheapest route, to be verified before launch.

  • Without XPR: Exeswap works as a regular aggregator. The user trades and pays the normal fee.
  • With XPR (opt-in): the user’s verified on-chain activity sets their XPR, and XPR redeems part of each trade’s fee.
  • Before signing: every quote shows the full fee, what XPR redeems and what the user pays (see “Your quote” in the Exeswap simulator). Once approved, that price is fixed.

Fees

Each trade costs the network’s gas plus Exe Protocol’s fee of 0.10% of the trade. XPR redeems a share of that total: at Tier 1, with the pool at full strength, XPR 0.60 redeems 60%.

Worked example: a $1,000 trade on a low-fee chain ($0.01 gas), Tier 1, pool at full strength. Simulated.

The user’s own fees can redeem up to 70% of the protocol fee they paid ($0.70 here); the pool covers the rest of what their XPR earns.

XPR

XPR (Exe Protocol Rate) is a wallet’s exchange rate, earned through verified on-chain activity. It runs from 0.00 to 1.00, and up to 1.10 at Tiers 2 and 3.

  • Belongs to the wallet: person or agent. It is calculated the same way everywhere and is designed to travel across every app using Exe Protocol.

  • Moves gradually: each month counts for 30% of the rate, so it follows recent activity without jumping overnight.

  • Higher tiers earn more from the same XPR. What a wallet’s XPR earns:

    earned = 1 − (1 − XPR) ^ (tier multiplier × surplus factor)

At Tier 1 with no surplus, that is exactly the XPR. Tier 1 is never above 1.00, including after falling back from a higher tier.

Tiers

Tiers belong to the user, not the app. Every wallet starts at Tier 1; higher tiers are earned, not bought.

Tier How to reach it Max XPR How it is lost
1 Everyone starts here 1.00 —
2 Set aside a budget in your own wallet: usually about 10% of a month’s trading (5% to 25%, lower for a long, steady history) 1.10 Budget commitment ends
3 Stay at Tier 2 with a smoothed XPR of 0.75 or more for four months in a row 1.10 Two months below 0.75: back to Tier 2

A budget above the minimum is headroom for a busier month only. It does not raise the rate: redemption is earned through signal, never bought.

Funding

Redemption is paid first from the user’s own fees, then from the Sponsorship Pool. Users’ own money never enters the pool.

  • Own fees: up to 70% of the protocol fee the user paid. This part is never cut, whatever the pool’s state.
  • The pool: funded by 70% of Exe Protocol’s revenue; the other 30% goes to operations. Each app has its own account in the pool, built from the fees its users pay, so no app subsidises another.
  • Pool strength: how much of what users’ XPR earns the pool can pay right now. At 100%, users redeem all of it. If the pool runs low, everyone’s pool share shrinks by the same proportion.
  • Surplus: when the pool holds more than it needs, the surplus lifts what Tiers 2 and 3 earn (the surplus factor above). Beyond a reserve of three months of redemption, the share earmarked for $XPT buybacks is held until legally cleared.
  • Under stress: in every stress we tested, the pool never ran dry; it steps down gracefully under load.

Safeguards

The rules are built so fresh wallets and fake activity cannot drain the pool.

  • New and risky wallets: wallets in their first months, or flagged last month, share a capped part of the pool: together at most 5% of its monthly allocation. Their own-fee redemption is unaffected.
  • Flags: a flagged wallet drops to Tier 1 for that month and receives no shared redemption. Its XPR history is kept, so once cleared it loses nothing in later months.
  • Caps on shared redemption: a daily fee cap ($50 a day) and a trade cap (500 trades) limit what the pool pays any one wallet. Own-fee redemption uses all trades.
  • Tested against farming: farm-defence and stress scenarios were run against the engine and reviewed.

Settlement and custody

Users’ money stays in their own wallets; Exe Protocol never holds it.

  • Tier budgets are set aside in the user’s own non-custodial wallet. The user keeps control of them.
  • Recorded at once, settled in batches: a trade’s fee redemption is recorded when the user approves the quote, then settled on-chain in batches to keep costs down.
  • Gas: paid through Exe Protocol’s paymaster, so redemption can cover gas as well as the protocol fee.

Any public claim about custody waits for legal advice.
